# RealPolitics Insights

# REALPOLITICS — MASTER FRONT-END PRODUCT & DESIGN PROMPT

Build a complete, premium, responsive front-end for a new educational platform called:

# REALPOLITICS

**Primary market:** United States

**Primary language:** English (US)

**Secondary language:** Spanish

**Platforms:** Web-first, fully responsive and designed so it can later become a mobile app/PWA.

RealPolitics is NOT merely a political-news application.

It is an ambitious educational platform designed to teach people the knowledge required to understand how society, economics, government, history, institutions, science, finance, technology, geopolitics and the modern world actually work.

The product should feel like a combination of:

* a premium learning platform,

* an interactive encyclopedia,

* a knowledge RPG,

* a modern civic academy,

* an interactive historical atlas,

* and a personalized learning map.

But it must have a completely original identity.

Do NOT make it look like a traditional school LMS.

Do NOT create something resembling a generic university portal.

Do NOT make politics dominate the entire product.

RealPolitics should feel like:

> "The app that teaches you how the world actually works."

# INTERACTIVE DECISION

Robinson has 10 hours of daylight.

He can spend those hours:

Fishing

Gathering coconuts

Building shelter

Making tools

Resting

Display a visual allocation slider.

Example:

Fishing — 4 hours

Coconuts — 2 hours

Shelter — 3 hours

Tools — 1 hour

Total:

10 / 10 HOURS

When the user increases one activity, they must decrease another.

Show a message:

**Resources are limited. Choices are unavoidable.**

Then introduce:

# Scarcity

Definition:

**Scarcity exists because resources are limited while human wants are greater than the resources available to satisfy them.**

Award:

+10 XP

---

# OPPORTUNITY COST SCENE

New illustrated panel.

Robinson can choose:

### Option A

Spend 3 hours fishing.

Expected result:

6 fish.

### Option B

Spend 3 hours improving his fishing spear.

Today:

0 fish.

Tomorrow:

12 fish.

Ask:

# What should Robinson do?

Let the learner choose.

There is no simplistic "correct" answer.

Explain:

The decision depends on:

* current food supply

* future productivity

* risk

* time preference

Introduce:

# Opportunity Cost

Definition:

**The opportunity cost of a decision is the value of the best alternative you give up.**

Visual example:

If Robinson spends three hours building a spear, the opportunity cost may be the fish he could have caught during those three hours.

---

# PRODUCTIVITY

Show Robinson using:

Hands:

2 fish/hour

Basic spear:

4 fish/hour

Improved spear:

6 fish/hour

Animated chart.

Introduce:

# Productivity

Output per unit of input.

Explain how tools and technology increase productivity.

---

# CAPITAL

The improved spear becomes a teaching example.

Introduce:

# Capital

In economics, capital includes productive assets used to produce other goods and services.

Important:

Explain that economic capital does not simply mean "money."

Show:

Spear → productive capital

Fishing net → productive capital

Shelter → partially productive asset

Coconuts → consumption good

---

# SAVING AND INVESTMENT

Scenario:

Robinson catches 8 fish.

He can eat:

8 today

or

Explain:

Consuming less today can allow resources to be used for future production.

Introduce:

Saving

Investment

Delayed consumption

Time preference

Do NOT overload the learner with equations.

Make this intuitive.

# 13. MINI EXAM

---

QUESTION 1

Robinson has four hours available. If he spends them building a shelter instead of fishing, the fish he could have caught represent:

A) Inflation

B) Opportunity cost

C) Capital gains

D) Interest

Correct:

B

After answering, explain why.

---

QUESTION 2

Robinson builds a fishing spear that allows him to catch more fish per hour.

The spear primarily increases:

A) Inflation

B) Productivity

C) Scarcity

D) Taxes

Correct:

B

---

QUESTION 3

Which best describes scarcity?

A) People never have enough money.

B) Resources are limited relative to human wants.

C) Governments restrict production.

D) Prices always rise.

Correct:

B

---

QUESTION 4

Robinson and Friday specialize in different tasks and exchange goods.

This demonstrates:

A) Specialization and trade

B) Monetary inflation

C) Public debt

D) Regulation

Correct:

A

---

QUESTION 5

Why might Robinson choose to spend time building a better fishing tool instead of catching fish today?

A) Because future productivity may increase.

B) Because fish have no economic value.

C) Because scarcity disappears.

D) Because opportunity cost becomes zero.

Correct:

A
